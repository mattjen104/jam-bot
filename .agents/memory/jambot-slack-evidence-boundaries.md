---
name: JamBot Slack evidence boundaries
description: How attributed Slack song shares and a privately paired Lore library may be used in channel answers.
---

**Rule:** In the public Slack channel, a shared song link is evidence that a speaker posted the link and, when present, what they wrote beside it. It is not evidence that they like, played, saved, or listened to the song. A speaker's explicit artist or genre statement can drive public radio recommendations, but another person's Lore library can only ground answers after its owner privately confirms a revocable channel-scoped grant. Never use an anonymous Lore device session as a substitute for that grant.

**Why:** The channel mixes different speakers and the Lore account is device-local. Conflating social posts with taste or guessing which device belongs to a Slack speaker would reveal private library information or misattribute an opinion.

**How to apply:** Keep speaker-specific answers tied to exact link identity and verified Slack user identity. Ask for a supported link or unambiguous mention rather than filling a gap with recent posts or a guessed title. For library crossings, distinguish saved recording, album, and artist-only evidence and keep transient unavailable/partial data separate from settled no-match results.
