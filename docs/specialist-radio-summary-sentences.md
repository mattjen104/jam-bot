# Specialist Radio: editorial summary sentences

Reviewed September 2, 2026. These are the sixteen non-SomaFM stations in the
final Specialist Radio cohort. Each sentence is intentionally useful even when
the station does not publish track-level play history: it explains the
editorial reason the station belongs in Lore without implying metadata that
the source does not provide.

1. **KEXP 90.3 FM — Seattle:** A human-curated global-music institution whose
   unusually rich public play history makes it both a dependable discovery
   source and a strong historical anchor for Specialist Radio.
2. **Kiosk Radio — Brussels:** An adventurous community station where
   underground club music, experimental electronics, and visiting selectors
   turn a small Brussels signal into a consistently surprising listening
   surface.
3. **Lahmacun Radio — Budapest:** A Budapest community station with a
   distinctly human mix of regional, experimental, and genre-crossing shows
   that broadens the specialist dial beyond the usual Anglo-American centers.
4. **Oroko Radio — Accra:** An Accra-led platform for African and diasporic
   electronic music, connecting local selectors and global listeners through
   an energetic, forward-looking programme.
5. **LYL Radio — Lyon:** A Lyon-based left-field station for ambient,
   experimental, and boundary-pushing selections that rewards listeners who
   want discovery rather than a predictable genre channel.
6. **8Ball Radio — New York:** A fiercely independent New York community
   station built around artist-led shows, local culture, and eclectic
   selectors whose value is the people and programming even when live
   track-level metadata is unavailable.
7. **Boxout FM — New Delhi:** A New Delhi platform documenting independent
   Indian and South Asian music across hip-hop, electronic, and alternative
   scenes, included as a vital regional perspective even without a verified
   live track feed.
8. **Cashmere Radio — Berlin:** A Berlin experimental station pairing
   underground music with talk, performance, and unusually broad guest
   programming, making its programme archive valuable even when it cannot
   honestly identify every live track.
9. **dublab — Los Angeles:** A long-running Los Angeles non-profit whose
   patient, exploratory programming treats ambient, jazz, beat, electronic,
   and global sounds as one expansive field of discovery.
10. **Rinse FM — London:** A foundational London voice for UK garage, grime,
    bass, and forward club music whose specialist identity is unmistakable
    even when its live stream exposes only placeholder metadata.
11. **Worldwide FM — London:** A global selector station shaped by DJs,
    musicians, and cultural curators, providing a wide-angle, human alternative
    to algorithmic world-music categorisation.
12. **Refuge Worldwide — Berlin:** A community-minded Berlin station that
    gives underrepresented selectors and border-crossing sounds a visible
    home, with its schedule and shows carrying the editorial signal when
    tracks are not published live.
13. **The Lot Radio — Brooklyn:** A Brooklyn all-DJ station whose intimate,
    continuously changing live format captures the feeling of being inside a
    real selector’s session rather than browsing a static genre playlist.
14. **Radio Nopal — Mexico City:** An independent Mexico City signal that
    brings a distinctly Latin American point of view to the dial and remains
    worth hearing even when its useful track identity depends on intermittent
    ICY metadata.
15. **NTS 1 — London:** One of London’s defining left-field channels, where
    specialist residents and guest selectors move freely across electronic,
    jazz, experimental, global, and archival music.
16. **NTS 2 — London:** A complementary NTS channel with its own programming
    identity, extending the network’s deep selector culture without reducing it
    to a single flagship sound.

## Editorial boundary

The four SomaFM channels are documented separately because their public
timestamped JSON feeds support repeatable shallow track-history ingestion.
The sentences above do not claim historical play coverage for the other
stations. Where a station publishes only shows, schedules, or recordings,
those sources support identity and programme discovery—not invented artist,
title, timestamp, or DJ attribution.
